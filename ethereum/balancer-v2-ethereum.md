## Architecture and execution map

- `Vault` is the singleton custody and settlement contract. Pools register token sets and specialization; the Vault stores each pool's cash and managed balances, user internal balances, and last-change block. The sampled two-token pool registers with `TWO_TOKEN` specialization and has no asset managers.
- Swaps enter `Vault.swap` or `Vault.batchSwap`. The Vault validates token and pool relationships, calls `onSwap`, applies returned amounts to its packed pool cash balances, aggregates batch deltas, checks caller limits, then settles positive deltas from the sender/internal balance and negative deltas to the recipient/internal balance. `batchSwap` supports chained steps by using a zero amount as the prior step's calculated amount. The Vault is non-reentrant across these state-changing routes.
- Liquidity enters `joinPool` and `exitPool`. The Vault validates that supplied assets exactly match registered tokens and order, calls the pool hook, enforces amount limits, transfers assets and protocol fees, updates cash, and mints/burns BPT through pool hooks. In Recovery Mode, pool exits take a simplified path that bypasses the normal weighted math.
- `WeightedPool2Tokens` stores immutable token weights and decimal scaling factors, BPT supply, swap fee, pause/recovery state, protocol-fee checkpoints, and optional price-oracle observations. `WeightedMath` computes the weighted product invariant and swap/join/exit amounts. For this pool, weights are 80/20 and the initial swap fee is 1%; authorized configuration may change pool swap fees within implementation bounds.
- Additional Vault entry points reviewed: `manageUserBalance` for internal balance deposit, withdrawal, and internal/external transfer; `flashLoan` with pre/post token balance reconciliation and fee collection; pool token registration/deregistration and asset-manager accounting; and protocol fee payment. These paths share the Vault reentrancy guard and authorization checks.

## Entry points and invariants reviewed

- Pool registration and pool ID/specialization; token registration, ordering, cash/managed balance packing, and deregistration conditions.
- `swap`, `batchSwap`, and `queryBatchSwap`; single-step and multihop amounts; given-in/given-out rounding; signed net deltas; limits; ETH/WETH and internal-balance settlement.
- `joinPool`, `exitPool`, normal and recovery-mode paths; BPT mint/burn, scaling and downscaling, protocol swap-fee BPT accounting, and external token transfers.
- `manageUserBalance`, flash loans, fee collection, pausing, recovery mode, pool authorization, and asset-manager balance changes.
- Invariants: Vault cash and managed totals represent pool claims; each swap's amount deltas are equal and opposite across the two pool assets after fee treatment; batch routes settle only net per-token deltas; pool cash changes match transfers and fees; BPT supply tracks the weighted invariant and liquidity claims; protocol fee checkpoints advance once per completed liquidity operation; flash-loan balances return to at least their pre-loan values plus fee; failed limits or settlement revert the full transaction.

## Findings

No confirmed implementation defect was identified in the shared Vault settlement paths or the sampled `WeightedPool2Tokens` weighted math and liquidity paths.

### Rejected hypothesis: multihop batching leaves intermediate assets unaccounted

Each pool swap updates its own Vault cash balance while `_swapWithPools` accumulates signed deltas by asset index. The Vault transfers only the final net-positive input and net-negative output after validating limits. A middle token consumed by a later step nets against the earlier step's output; any nonzero residual remains in the final delta and is settled. The operation is under `nonReentrant`, and any failed limit or transfer reverts all pool balance writes. This matches the intended batch-swap accounting model, so no independent accounting defect was established.

### Rejected hypothesis: pool invariant view exposes transient Vault state

`BaseWeightedPool.getInvariant()` reads balances from the Vault and documents that a call made during a join/exit context can observe an inconsistent state; it directs callers to use `VaultReentrancyLib.ensureNotInVaultContext`. This is an explicit caller-facing view limitation. The sampled BAL/WETH weighted pool's reviewed swap hook has no token-transfer callback before Vault balances are written, and no exploitable DEX accounting path was demonstrated. Integrations that use pool views during arbitrary token callbacks require separate assessment.

Other reviewed boundaries did not produce a credible candidate: swap in/out is limited to 30% of the respective balance; normalized weights must sum to one and each meet the minimum; given-in amounts round output down while given-out amounts round input up; join and exit scales round in the pool-favoring direction; batch array indexes and route continuation are checked; internal-balance debits are capped or require sufficient funds according to operation; and flash-loan token lists must be strictly sorted, preventing duplicate-token accounting.

## Validation and limitations

- **2025 incident cross-check:** Balancer's Nov. 18, 2025 postmortem attributes the Nov. 3 incident to downward rounding in Stable Pool exact-output math combined with rate-provider imprecision and low liquidity; composable BPT swaps enabled the low-liquidity state. It reports $94.8M stolen and $121.1M total losses across Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base, Optimism, and Polygon. It explicitly says Weighted Pools were unaffected. This incident shows the excluded Composable Stable family needs a separate audit; it does not establish a defect in the sampled `WeightedPool2Tokens`. The supplied $128M/six-chain figure and read-only-reentrancy explanation do not match the protocol's [postmortem](https://medium.com/balancer-protocol/nov-3-exploit-post-mortem-51dcbeb6b020).
- **Rejected read-only-reentrancy chain:** the postmortem describes `exitSwap` and repeated exact-output swaps through composable BPT operations to magnify rounding loss. It does not describe an integrator borrowing against a transient `getInvariant()` view during a callback. The reviewed `getInvariant()` limitation remains a separate integration concern, but this incident does not validate the hypothesized chain.

- No custom regression test was written or run because no credible invariant violation remained. The relevant repository test sources were not run as a full suite.
- Read-only explorer metadata and the GeckoTerminal pool page establish the sampled contract identity, token pair, Vault link, compiler settings, weights, and fee snapshot. No state-changing calls were made to public deployments.
- This report does not claim a complete review of every pool implementation listed under Balancer V2. In particular, stable/composable-stable pools, managed pools, LBPs, recovery-specific implementation differences, external pool contracts, token behavior, and downstream consumers of the price oracle require their own source-level review.
